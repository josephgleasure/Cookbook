Ginger Pork Hot Pot

Serves 8Total 2.5 to 3 hoursHands on ~35 minutesKeeps 4 days
Home

Ingredients

Large-batch, 3 to 4 days of leftovers
  • 1.5 kg boneless pork shoulder, cut into 4 to 5 cm cubes
  • 250 g fresh ginger, peeled
  • 300 g garlic chives, cut into 8 cm lengths
  • 2 litres dashi stock, plus extra hot water if needed
  • 120 ml shiro dashi
  • 60 ml light soy sauce
  • 1/2 tsp fine sea salt, plus more to taste
  • Cooked rice or udon, for serving (optional)

Steps

  1. Slice 100 g of the ginger into thin coins. Finely julienne the remaining 150 g, put it in a bowl of cold water, and soak for 15 minutes to soften its sharp edge. Drain well.
  2. Put the dashi, shiro dashi, soy sauce, salt, and sliced ginger in a large heavy pot. Bring to a boil.
  3. Add the pork in two or three batches so the broth keeps simmering. Skim off the foam and excess surface fat as it rises.
  4. Turn the heat to low. Cover with the lid slightly ajar and simmer very gently for 2 hours, turning the cubes every 30 minutes. Keep the pork mostly submerged, adding a little hot water if the broth drops too far.
  5. Test a large cube: it should yield easily to a fork or chopstick but still hold its shape. If it is firm, simmer for another 15 to 30 minutes. Taste the broth and adjust with salt or soy sauce.
  6. For tonight's meal, move the number of pork portions you need and enough broth to cover them into a smaller saucepan. Bring to a simmer, then add the same share of garlic chives and drained julienned ginger. Cook for 3 to 5 minutes, until the chives wilt and the ginger softens slightly.
  7. Serve piping hot in bowls, on its own or with rice. Udon can be simmered directly in the broth for a heartier meal.
  8. For leftovers, keep the unused chives and julienned ginger separate. Cool the pork and broth promptly, portion into shallow containers, and refrigerate for up to 4 days. Reheat only the portion you need to a full simmer, then add fresh chives and ginger for the final 3 to 5 minutes.

Adapted for large pork shoulder cubes and batch cooking from RecipeTin Japan's Ginger Hot Pot with Pork. Pork shoulder needs the long, gentle simmer that the original thin pork slices do not.
