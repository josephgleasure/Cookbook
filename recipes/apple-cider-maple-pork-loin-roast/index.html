Apple Cider & Maple Pork Loin Roast

Serves 2Thermometer-drivenFinish at 145°F / 63°CSource-faithful
Home
Quantity:

Thanksgiving dinner for 2 using the purchased 1.1 kg boneless pork loin, sirloin half.

Ingredients

Thanksgiving · serves 2
  • 1.1 kg boneless pork loin, sirloin half, tied into an even shape
  • 2 sprigs fresh rosemary
  • 2 sprigs fresh thyme
  • 35 g unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 tbsp + 2 tsp pure maple syrup
  • 110 ml dry apple cider
  • 1 small red onion, quartered
  • 2⅔ cups Brussels sprouts, trimmed and halved
  • Olive oil, for searing
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
Christmas · serves 8
  • 2 kg boneless pork loin, tied
  • 3–4 sprigs fresh rosemary
  • 3–4 sprigs fresh thyme
  • 60 g unsalted butter, softened
  • 50 ml pure maple syrup
  • 200 ml dry apple cider
  • 2 small red onions, quartered
  • 5 cups Brussels sprouts, trimmed and halved
  • Olive oil, for searing
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste

Cooking time changes with the roast’s shape and thickness. Use the temperature stages below, not the clock alone.

Steps

  1. Take the chill off. Remove the roast from the refrigerator about 30 minutes before cooking. Pat it dry, tie if needed for an even shape, and season generously with salt and black pepper.
  2. Heat the oven. Preheat to 350°F / 175°C with a rack near the centre.
  3. Sear every side. Heat a little olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Brown the pork for about 2–3 minutes per side, then transfer it to an oven-safe roasting vessel.
  4. Add herbs, butter, and maple. Strip and roughly chop the rosemary and thyme. Press them onto the pork, spread the softened butter over the surface, and brush with the maple syrup.
  5. Add cider and roast to 120°F / 49°C. Pour the dry cider around—not over—the pork. Roast uncovered, occasionally spooning pan juices over the meat. Begin checking at about 30 minutes; the roast’s shape and thickness determine the actual time.Begin checking at about 35 minutes.
  6. Add the vegetables. Once the pork reaches 120°F / 49°C, arrange the red onion and Brussels sprouts around it and toss them through the pan juices. Return the uncovered roast to the oven.
  7. Roast to 135°F / 57°C. Continue until the thickest part of the pork reaches 135°F / 57°C and the vegetables are tender. The source allows about 20–30 more minutes; start checking the smaller Thanksgiving roast sooner.
  8. Rest, covered. Cover the vessel and rest the pork for 15 minutes, verifying that the centre reaches 145°F / 63°C before slicing.
  9. Finish and serve. Remove the twine, slice the pork, and serve with the roasted onions and Brussels sprouts. Spoon the cider-maple pan juices over everything.
