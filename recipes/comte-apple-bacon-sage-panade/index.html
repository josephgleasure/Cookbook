Comté, Apple, Bacon & Sage Panade

Serves 8Prep 40 minutesBake 60–70 minutes9 × 13-inch dish
Home

Adjusted liquid ratio: use 750 ml total liquid for 450 g dried bread (5:3 by weight). Add 650 ml first and keep 100 ml in reserve; use it only on dry patches after the soak. The panade should be saturated and yielding, not swimming.

Ingredients

  • 450 g sturdy country or sourdough bread, cut into 2.5 cm cubes and dried overnight (or 15 minutes in a 150°C / 300°F oven)
  • 225 g thick-cut bacon, cut crosswise into 1 cm pieces
  • 2 large yellow onions, thinly sliced
  • 1 large crisp-tart apple (such as Honeycrisp or Pink Lady), cored and cut into 1 cm cubes
  • 2 tbsp finely chopped fresh sage, plus a few leaves for the top
  • 2 garlic cloves, finely minced
  • 500 ml low-sodium chicken stock
  • 250 ml whole milk or half-and-half
  • 300 g Comté, coarsely grated and divided
  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter, for the baking dish and top
  • ½ tsp fine salt and ½ tsp black pepper, plus more to taste

Steps

  1. Dry the bread until firm at the edges but not deeply toasted. Butter a 9 × 13-inch (23 × 33 cm) baking dish.
  2. Cook the bacon in a large skillet over medium heat until browned but still a little flexible, 7 to 9 minutes. Transfer it with a slotted spoon, leaving about 2 tbsp fat in the pan.
  3. Add the onions and salt. Cook over medium-low heat for 15 to 18 minutes, stirring often, until soft and golden. Add the apple and cook 4 minutes; add the sage and garlic and cook 1 minute more. Season with pepper.
  4. Warm the stock and milk together until steaming. Reserve 100 ml. In a large bowl, gently toss the bread with the remaining 650 ml liquid, onion-apple mixture, bacon, and 200 g of the Comté.
  5. Spoon into the buttered dish without packing it down hard. Press lightly so the bread meets the liquid. Rest 20 minutes. Check several spots: add the reserved liquid only where bread still feels dry, a spoonful at a time.
  6. Scatter over the remaining 100 g Comté, dot with butter, and add a few sage leaves. Cover tightly with foil. Bake at 170°C / 335°F (150°C fan) for 35 minutes.
  7. Uncover and bake another 25 to 30 minutes, until bubbling at the edges, deeply golden on top, and hot in the centre. Rest 10 minutes before serving.
  8. Single-oven lamb schedule: during the final 35 minutes of the lamb’s 4½-hour covered roast at 170°C, put the covered panade on a separate rack. When the lamb is turned and uncovered for browning, uncover the panade too. Bake both for another 25 to 30 minutes; remove the panade when golden and let the lamb continue to its required browning time if needed. The panade rests while you finish the gravy.
  9. For make-ahead, assemble through the soak, cover, and refrigerate up to 1 day. Let it stand at room temperature for 30 minutes before baking. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours for up to 4 days; reheat covered at 170°C until steaming, then uncover briefly to re-crisp.

Developed for the Christmas dinner menu. The shared-oven timing follows RecipeTin Eats’ Slow Roast Leg of Lamb: 4½ hours covered at 170°C, followed by uncovered browning and a rest.
