Comté, Apple, Bacon & Sage Panade

Serves 8 as a sidePrep 45 minutesBake 75–85 minutes2.8-litre casserole
Home

Faithful, adjustable ratio: the bread, stock, onion, garlic, and cheese follow the 101 Cookbooks formula, scaled linearly to 200 g Comté for 8 side servings. Apple, bacon, and sage scale by the same multiplier. Pour the stock slowly and stop just below the top if your bread needs less.

1× recipe

Ingredients

  • 300 g day-old, sturdy artisan sourdough, crusts on, torn into rough 2.5 cm cubes
  • 150 g thick-cut bacon, cut crosswise into 1 cm pieces
  • 800 g yellow onions, thinly sliced
  • 1 large apple, crisp-tart (such as Honeycrisp or Pink Lady), cored and cut into 1 cm cubes
  • 2 tbsp finely chopped fresh sage, plus a few leaves for the top
  • 6 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
  • 1040 ml good-quality low-sodium chicken stock, plus a little extra kept hot if needed
  • 200 g Comté, coarsely grated and divided
  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter, for the casserole and top
  • 0.5 tsp fine salt and 0.5 tsp black pepper, plus more to taste

Steps

  1. Heat the oven to 165°C / 325°F (145°C fan). Butter a deep 2.8-litre casserole or Dutch oven.
  2. Cook the bacon in a large skillet over medium heat until browned but still a little flexible, 7 to 9 minutes. Transfer it with a slotted spoon, leaving about 2 tbsp fat in the pan.
  3. Add the onions and salt. Cook over medium-low heat for 25 to 30 minutes, stirring often, until soft, jammy, and golden. Add the apple and cook 4 minutes; add the sage and garlic and cook 2 minutes more. Season with pepper.
  4. Warm the stock to a simmer and taste it—it should be well seasoned. Toss the bread with 100 ml of the hot stock, then gently squeeze and turn the cubes so they are evenly moistened.
  5. Layer one-third of the onion-apple mixture in the casserole, followed by half the bread, half the bacon, and one-third of the Comté. Repeat, then finish with the remaining onion mixture and Comté. Press lightly so everything fits, without compacting it hard.
  6. Slowly pour the remaining hot stock around the edges in several additions. Pause for a minute between additions so the bread can absorb it; stop when the liquid reaches just below the top. Dot with butter and add a few sage leaves.
  7. Set the casserole over low heat until the edges begin to bubble. Remove from the heat, cover with parchment and then foil or a lid, and bake for 60 minutes. Put a sheet pan on the rack below in case it bubbles over.
  8. Uncover, raise the oven to 190°C / 375°F (170°C fan), and bake another 15 to 25 minutes, until bubbling at the edges and deeply golden on top. Add a splash of hot stock if the centre seems dry. Rest 10 minutes before serving.
  9. Single-oven lamb schedule: bake the covered panade beside the lamb for the final 60 minutes of the lamb’s 4½-hour covered roast at 170°C. When the lamb is uncovered for browning, uncover the panade too. The panade should brown in 15 to 25 minutes; remove it when golden and let it rest while the lamb finishes and you make the gravy.
  10. For make-ahead, prepare the bacon and onion-apple mixture up to 1 day ahead, then assemble and add the hot stock shortly before baking. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours for up to 4 days; reheat covered at 170°C until steaming, then uncover briefly to re-crisp.

Adapted from 101 Cookbooks’ Scallion & Chive Panade, scaled around 200 g Comté and varied with apple, bacon, and sage. The shared-oven timing follows RecipeTin Eats’ Slow Roast Leg of Lamb: 4½ hours covered at 170°C, followed by uncovered browning and a rest.
