Comté, Apple, Bacon & Sage Panade — TEST Version

Serves 8 as a sidePrep 45 minutesBake 65–70 minutesTEST recipe
Home

Current TEST version: use 750 ml hot chicken stock initially and keep up to 250 ml more available. Add it gradually and stop according to the bread: the centre should be very moist and almost spoonable, while the top stays deeply browned and crisp. Record how much stock the bread actually accepts.

1× recipe

Ingredients

  • 450 g day-old crusty country bread or sourdough, torn into rough 3 to 4 cm pieces
  • 200 g bacon, cut into lardons
  • 2 medium onions, thinly sliced
  • 2 tart apples, peeled, cored, and cut into 1.5 cm pieces
  • 10 sage leaves to 12 sage leaves, chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 125 ml dry white wine
  • 750 ml hot chicken stock initially, plus up to 250 ml additional hot stock as needed
  • 200 g Comté, coarsely grated and divided
  • 30 g unsalted butter, for the onions and casserole
  • Fine salt and black pepper, as needed

Steps

  1. Heat the oven to 175°C / 350°F (155°C fan). Spread the bread on sheet pans and dry it for 8 to 10 minutes; it should feel dry but not deeply toasted. Butter a roughly 3-litre casserole.
  2. Cook the bacon slowly in a large skillet until browned but not completely crisp, 7 to 9 minutes. Transfer it with a slotted spoon, leaving about 2 tbsp fat in the pan.
  3. Add the butter, onions, and a small pinch of salt. Cook over medium-low heat for 25 to 30 minutes, stirring often, until very soft and beginning to caramelize.
  4. Add the apples and cook for 3 to 4 minutes so they soften at the edges but remain identifiable. Add the sage and garlic for the final 2 minutes. Pour in the white wine and reduce it by about two-thirds, then stir the bacon back in and season with black pepper.
  5. Build loose layers in the casserole: bread, apple-bacon-onion mixture, and Comté; repeat, then finish with bread and a generous scattering of Comté. Press only lightly so the layers settle without becoming compact.
  6. Warm the chicken stock. Slowly pour in 750 ml around the edges in several additions, pausing between additions so the bread can absorb it. Press gently, assess the centre, and add up to 250 ml more as needed. Record the amount used.
  7. Reduce the oven to 165°C / 325°F (145°C fan). Cover tightly and bake for 45 minutes.
  8. Uncover and bake for another 20 to 25 minutes, until deeply browned and crisp on top while the centre remains very moist. Rest for 10 minutes before serving.
  9. Make ahead: on Christmas Eve, stop the uncovered stage after about 10 minutes. Cool promptly, cover, and refrigerate. On Christmas Day, reheat covered at 175°C / 350°F for about 20 minutes, then uncover for 15 to 20 minutes to finish browning. Spoon a few tablespoons of stock around the edge first if it looks dry.
  10. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours for up to 4 days. Reheat covered until steaming throughout, then uncover briefly to re-crisp the top.

Adapted from the classic layered panade method represented by 101 Cookbooks’ Scallion & Chive Panade, with Comté, tart apple, bacon, sage, and a deliberately variable stock quantity for the November test.
