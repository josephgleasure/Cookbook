Saint-Jacques à la Bretonne

Serves 44 shallow portions
Home
Quantity:

Thanksgiving test with Cognac and stock.

Ingredients

  • 400 g large scallops, thawed if frozen and patted dry
  • 2 shallot(s)
  • 5 white mushrooms
  • 1 small carrot(s)
  • 1 leek white(s)
  • 1 small celery stalk(s)
  • 25 g all-purpose flour
  • 60 g lightly salted butter, divided
  • 2 tbsp (30 ml) Cognac
  • 470 ml vegetable stock or fish stock
  • 1 tbsp thick full-fat crème fraîche, optional
  • 4 tbsp fine breadcrumbs
  • White pepper and fleur de sel, to taste

Steps

  1. Preheat to 200°C / about 400°F convection. Set out shallow oven-safe glass dishes with enough room for the scallops to sit in a single layer. Use fully preheated convection bake and keep glass out from under the broiler.
  2. Finely dice the shallot, carrot, leek and celery. Slice the mushrooms. Sweat the diced vegetables in 40 g of the butter until softened, then add the mushrooms and cook until they shrink and release their moisture.
  3. Measure 170 ml of the stock into a separate cup. Take the pan off the heat, add the measured Cognac, then add that 170 ml of stock. Return to the heat and reduce until the pan is nearly dry. Reserve the remaining 300 ml of stock for the sauce.
  4. Stir the flour and white pepper through the vegetables. Gradually stir in the reserved stock and simmer for about 10 minutes, or until the carrot is tender. Stir in the crème fraîche if using. Taste for salt. If you skip the crème fraîche, a few drops of lemon juice can brighten the finished sauce.
  5. Divide the raw scallops among the glass dishes in a single layer. Spoon the sauce over them, scatter on the breadcrumbs and dot with the remaining 20 g of butter.
  6. Bake until the topping is golden and the scallops are opaque throughout. Anne Reverdy’s recipe card gives about 15 minutes. Check at 12 minutes for your large scallops. Serve the portions you plan to eat now.

Adapted from Anne Reverdy’s Coquilles Saint-Jacques à la Bretonne. The Thanksgiving version replaces the scaled vermouth with Cognac and stock. The earlier oven checks account for differences in scallop size and ramekins.

Leftovers

Refrigerate cooked portions in shallow containers within two hours. Eat within two to three days or freeze. Reheat only the portion you plan to eat to 74°C / 165°F. Avoid moving chilled glass straight into a hot oven. Health Canada leftover guidance
