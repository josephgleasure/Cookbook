Saint-Jacques à la Bretonne

Serves 8 as a first course8 ramekinsBake 15–20 minutesTEST recipe
Home

November test: the 100 g butter + 100 g flour + 500 ml milk béchamel is deliberately very thick. Test it as written before adjusting it. Also test whether fully assembled ramekins hold well overnight. Cool the filling completely before it touches the raw scallops and keep the ramekins cold until baking.

Ingredients

  • 300 g scallops, side muscles removed and patted dry
  • 2 carrots, cut into fine julienne
  • 2 leeks, thoroughly washed and finely sliced
  • 1 tbsp salted butter
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste
  • Fine fresh or lightly dried breadcrumbs, as needed
  • Béchamel: 100 g salted butter
  • Béchamel: 100 g plain flour
  • Béchamel: 500 ml whole milk
  • Béchamel: ½ fish stock cube or 1 tsp fish bouillon
  • Béchamel: freshly grated nutmeg, to taste

Steps

  1. Prepare the carrots and leeks. Melt 1 tbsp salted butter in a wide pan, add the vegetables, season lightly, cover, and cook gently for up to 10 minutes, until softened but not browned.
  2. Make the béchamel: melt 100 g butter in a saucepan, stir in the flour, and cook for 1 to 2 minutes without browning. Gradually whisk in the milk, then add the fish stock or bouillon and nutmeg. Cook, whisking, until smooth and very thick.
  3. Fold the softened carrot and leek mixture into the béchamel. Taste cautiously before adding salt—the salted butter and fish bouillon may already provide enough. Let the mixture cool completely if assembling ahead.
  4. Heat the oven to 180°C / 350°F. Divide the raw scallops among 8 ramekins. Spoon the cooled vegetable béchamel over them and finish with a thin, even layer of breadcrumbs.
  5. For the make-ahead test, cover the assembled ramekins and refrigerate overnight. Keep them cold until the oven is ready; do not leave raw scallops at room temperature.
  6. Bake for 15 to 20 minutes, until the filling is bubbling, the breadcrumbs are browned, and the scallops are opaque and cooked through. Serve immediately.

Adapted to 8 individual ramekins from Manon Lagrève’s user-supplied Saint-Jacques à la Bretonne recipe. The original béchamel ratio is intentionally preserved for testing.
